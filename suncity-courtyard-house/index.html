The Courtyard House

Two complete homes for two brothers, stacked one above the other, with the parents and the shared family spaces on the ground floor. One stone frame, one gate and one garden courtyard make it read as a single house from the street.

How it works

A

Two real homes. Each upper floor is a full house of about 2,400 sq ft with its own lockable main door, kitchen, living and four bedrooms. A shared stair and lift reach every floor.

B

Equal for both brothers. Stacking gives both houses the same plan, the same view over the Suncity garden and the same area. A side-by-side split would leave each house only 20 ft wide.

C

Parents in the middle of family life. They stay on the ground floor next to the courtyard and family dining, with no stairs to climb. Both sons are one lift ride away.

D

A courtyard at the heart. A 16' × 16' garden open to the sky sits in the centre of the plot (the Brahmasthan). It brings light and air into all three floors.

Design intent

Light, nature and restraint

Light everywhere

  • The open courtyard lights the dining, gallery and bedrooms of every floor from the middle of the plan.
  • Full-height glass faces the society garden on the west.
  • The north wall gets long windows for soft, glare-free daylight. North light is the best working light.
  • A skylight over the stair pulls light down three floors.

Taming the west sun

  • 4 ft deep balconies on both upper floors shade the glass from the high afternoon sun.
  • Vertical teak louvres in front of the bedrooms cut the low evening sun and keep privacy.
  • Planter hedges on every balcony cool the air before it enters.
  • Hot air rises out through the courtyard (stack ventilation), so the house needs less AC.

Nature inspired

  • A champa (frangipani) tree in the courtyard, visible from all three homes.
  • Kota stone floors and lime plaster walls, with a Jodhpur sandstone frame. All are local Rajasthan materials.
  • Roof lawn, herb beds and a kitchen garden, plus a rear garden with a swing for the parents.
  • Rainwater harvesting pit (required in Jaipur for plots over 300 m²) and a solar array on the roof.

Minimal by design

  • Few materials: white plaster, sandstone, teak and glass.
  • Flush storage walls. No false ceilings, so the full 12' height stays.
  • Clean rectangular rooms that are easy to furnish. No decorative mouldings or arches.

One house from outside

  • A single sandstone frame wraps all three floors.
  • One gate, one portico, one continuous balcony line and one pergola on the roof.
  • Utility areas face the rear, so the street side stays calm.

Vastu notes

  • West-facing main entrance. The courtyard keeps the centre (Brahmasthan) open.
  • Master bedroom and parents' bedroom sit in the south-west. Kitchens sit in the south-east.
  • Puja room is in the north-east zone of the ground floor.
  • The stair core is on the north side. If your vastu consultant prefers south, the whole plan can be mirrored.

Assumptions to confirm with your architect

  • Setbacks are taken as front 15', rear 10' and 5' on each side. Ground coverage is about 58%. Check the JDA bylaws and Suncity society rules for your plot; if they ask for wider setbacks, the plan narrows but the layout stays the same.
  • Height is about 44'6" to the parapet (13.6 m), below the 15 m limit that triggers high-rise fire rules.
  • A 30' road between the plot and the garden is assumed.
  • 50' × 90' works out to 500 sq yd. The 495 sq yd on paper suggests a small corner cut, which does not affect this layout.
  • This is a concept for discussion. Structure, services and legal drawings must be prepared by a licensed architect and structural engineer.
